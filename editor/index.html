The dirt fills itself in: under ground facing up, over a ceiling.

Alt-click picks up a tile · Shift-click moves the start

Drag a box. Drag inside it to move it; Alt-drag to move a copy. Arrow keys nudge it a tile.

A pasted or duplicated area follows the pointer until you click to put it down. A moved or pasted area floats over the stage (it isn't part of it yet) until you click outside it, press Enter or switch tools, so you can keep moving it without wiping what's underneath.

Tiles put down by hand sit over the tiles paths make. Moving, clearing or covering a path's own ground pieces breaks the path there; covering the dirt under it doesn't.

Click to add points; the tiles follow the curve, joining the ground either side, with fill chosen so the dirt's edge carries on from tile to tile. Where a click would add a point, a + shows: at the nearer end of the path, or on the curve itself.

The ground is on the side with the brown ticks: below a path drawn left to right. A path can go any way: up a wall, back over itself for an overhang, upside down along a ceiling. Turned tiles are used where it needs them.

Drag a point or a handle to reshape. Alt-drag a handle to make a corner: its handles then move on their own (the point shows as a diamond) until you Alt-click the point to line them up again. Double-click a point to make it smooth, right-click to remove it.

Right-click away from the points (or press Esc) to finish this path. Click another path's point to edit that path. A path's tiles go with it: move or delete it and they go too. Ground above a gap of sky (an overhang) is left alone.

The ground's shape follows as you drag; the art around it catches up a moment after you stop.

Drag a box to draw the shape. Shift keeps it as wide as it's tall.

A shape is a path that goes all the way round: reshape it with the Path tool (G), and Swap ground side turns a lump into a hole.

Click (or drag) where the truck drops in.

Click where the finish line goes; drag to move it. With none, the stage goes on until you quit, like a stunt track.

Stage Editor: keyboard and mouse

Tools

B PaintDrag paints the chosen tile, right-drag erases. Picking a tile from the tiles switches to Paint. Alt-click picks up a tile. H mirrors the chosen tile, V turns it upside down, R turns it a quarter (Shift+R the other way).
S SelectDrag a box; drag inside to move it, Alt-drag to copy. H flips it left to right, V upside down, R turns it; arrows nudge it, ⌘D duplicates it (click to put the copy down), Del clears it, Enter drops it, Esc lets go.
G PathClick to add points to a curve; the tiles follow it, the ground on the side with the brown ticks (any way round: walls, overhangs, ceilings). Drag points and handles; Alt-drag a handle for a corner, Alt-click the point to line its handles up again. Right-click removes a point; right-click elsewhere or Esc finishes the path.
Q ShapeDrag a box for a circle, square or diamond of ground (ground out), or a hole that shape (ground in). Shift keeps it round.
T StartClick where the truck drops in (or Shift-click while painting).
F FinishClick where the finish line goes.

Everywhere

Pinch, ⌘/Ctrl+scrollZoom at the pointer. + − zoom, 0 fits the height.
Scroll, Space+drag, middle-dragMove around. The strip at the bottom shows the whole level: click it to jump.
⌘C ⌘X ⌘VCopy, cut, paste (the pasted area floats under the pointer until you drop it).
⌘Z ⌘⇧ZUndo, redo.
Shift+arrowsMove the whole level a tile.
' C NGrid, collision lines, tile numbers.

⌘ is Ctrl on Windows and Linux. Tiles are 64 px; 1 tile is 2.5 m. The stage you're editing is kept in this browser as you go.
